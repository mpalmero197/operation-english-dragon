# Practice Brief — Focus on the Learner

**Pack practice only** (not an official Cambridge brief). Typical real length: **750–1000 words**.  
**Guide:** [Lesson 10](../../lessons/10-written-assignments-guide.md) · **Syllabus Topic:** 1 (+ links to 2/3)

---

## Scenario (original)

You have access to notes about **one learner** in a face-to-face adult evening class:

**Name:** Leo (he/him) · **Level:** B1 · **L1:** Brazilian Portuguese  
**Age:** 34 · **Job:** Junior project coordinator at a logistics firm  
**Goals:** Lead short stand-up updates in English; write clearer emails to UK partners  
**Preferences:** Likes pair speaking; dislikes long metalanguage lectures; remembers better with examples  
**Observed strengths:** Willing to speak; good listening gist  
**Observed needs:** Present perfect vs past simple in updates; email openings/closings; /θ/ in *think/Thursday*; article use with job nouns  
**Context:** Mixed L1 class of 11; 90-minute lessons twice weekly; coursebook + teacher-made materials

---

## Your tasks

1. **Summarise** Leo’s background, motivation, and preferences (use terminology accurately).  
2. **Identify** two language system needs and one skills need with brief evidence.  
3. **Recommend** two specific activities/resources (one systems-focused, one skills-focused) with **rationale** tied to Leo.  
4. **Reference** at least one free source (e.g. TeachingEnglish article theme, ELT Concourse page, learner dictionary) in a simple academic style.  
5. Keep clear, accurate English; aim 750–1000 words if simulating full length (or 400+ for a shorter drill).

## Mini sample rationale (do not copy as your whole assignment)

“A stand-up role-play with time limits suits Leo’s work goal and preference for speaking. Providing stems such as *I’ve already…* / *Yesterday I…* targets his tense confusion without a long lecture, matching his dislike of heavy metalanguage.”
