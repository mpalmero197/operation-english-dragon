# Week 2 Checkpoint Quiz — Grammar for Teachers

**Pace:** after Week 2 (~1–1.5 hrs/day) · **Lesson:** [02-grammar-for-teachers](../lessons/02-grammar-for-teachers.md) · **Qs:** 16

---

## Questions

**1.** Tense vs aspect — short definition of each (1 sentence each).

**2.** MCQ: *I’ve been waiting for an hour* primarily highlights:  
A. a finished past habit  B. duration leading to now  C. a future arrangement  D. passive voice

**3.** Form breakdown of present perfect continuous (affirmative).

**4.** Two CCQs for *You don’t have to wear a tie.*

**5.** Two CCQs for *You mustn’t wear a tie.* (contrast)

**6.** Rewrite passively: *The committee will announce the results tomorrow.*

**7.** Why might *I am knowing the answer* be incorrect in standard teaching models?

**8.** Label: inductive guided discovery or deductive — “Teacher gives rule for *for/since*, then examples.”

**9.** Mini analysis: *They might join us later.* — meaning (possibility), form, one problem.

**10.** Marker sentence + form for past simple passive.

**11.** Match: (1) unfinished time (2) past habit no longer true (3) prohibition — to: *used to* / *mustn’t* / present perfect with *this week*.

**12.** Write one subsidiary aim for a lesson whose main aim is present perfect for experience.

**13.** Error: *She can to cook well.* — correction + brief reason.

**14.** Name three modal meanings (not forms).

**15.** What belongs in language analysis on a CELTA plan besides meaning and form?

**16.** In 3–4 sentences: how does grammar analysis on CELTA differ from a linguistics essay?

---

## Answer key

**1.** Tense ≈ time reference location; aspect ≈ how the situation is viewed (progressive/perfect, etc.).  
**2.** B  
**3.** *have/has + been + verb-ing*  
**4.** Is it necessary? *(No)* Is it banned? *(No)*  
**5.** Is it allowed? *(No)* Is it against the rules? *(Yes)*  
**6.** *The results will be announced (by the committee) tomorrow.*  
**7.** *Know* is typically stative; use *I know*.  
**8.** Deductive  
**9.** Meaning: possibility (not certainty). Form: *might + bare infinitive*. Problem: treating *might* as past of *may* only; over-certainty with *will*.  
**10.** Example: *The window was broken.* Form: *was/were + past participle*.  
**11.** (1) present perfect + *this week* (2) *used to* (3) *mustn’t*  
**12.** Example: fluency in asking follow-ups; pronunciation of *Have you ever…?*  
**13.** *She can cook well* — modal + bare infinitive.  
**14.** Any three: ability, permission, obligation, advice, possibility, prohibition…  
**15.** Phonology (and typically problems/solutions, use/context).  
**16.** Teaching analysis is selective, contextualised, level-appropriate, aimed at clarification/practice — not exhaustive theoretical description.
