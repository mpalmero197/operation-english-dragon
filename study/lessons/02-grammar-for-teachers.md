## Core concepts

### 1. Grammar for *teaching*, not linguistics exams

On CELTA you analyse language so learners can **understand and use** it. Depth should match the level and the aim. A good analysis answers:

- **Meaning / use:** What idea does this express? When do we choose it?  
- **Form:** How is it built? (patterns, endings, word order, auxiliaries)  
- **Phonology:** How is it typically said? (weak forms, contractions, stress)  

This M–F–P trio appears again and again in plans and *Language related tasks*.

### 2. Building blocks (quick map)

| Area | Examples you must handle comfortably |
|------|--------------------------------------|
| **Word classes** | noun, verb, adjective, adverb, determiner, pronoun, preposition, conjunction |
| **Phrases** | noun phrase (*the old hotel*), verb phrase (*has been waiting*) |
| **Clauses / sentences** | main vs subordinate; simple / compound / complex |
| **Tense** | locating situation in time (present / past / future *reference*) |
| **Aspect** | how the situation is viewed (simple, progressive/continuous, perfect) |
| **Voice** | active vs passive |
| **Modality** | ability, permission, obligation, possibility, advice (*can, must, should, might…*) |
| **Countability** | countable / uncountable nouns; article implications |

### 3. Tense and aspect — teaching-friendly view

English verb phrases combine **time reference** and **aspect**. Learners often mix labels (“present tense”) with forms (*is eating*).

| Form (example) | Typical meaning focus for teaching |
|----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Present simple — *She works nights* | Habits, routines, facts, schedules |
| Present continuous — *She’s working tonight* | Temporary / in-progress / arrangements (context matters) |
| Past simple — *They arrived late* | Completed past events (often with past time marker) |
| Past continuous — *I was cooking when…* | Background / interrupted past actions |
| Present perfect simple — *I’ve lost my keys* | Past event with present relevance / experience / unfinished time |
| Present perfect continuous — *I’ve been waiting for an hour* | Activity duration leading to now; sometimes visible result |
| *will* — *I’ll email you* | Decisions at speaking time; predictions; offers (among uses) |
| *going to* — *I’m going to email you* | Prior intention / prediction from present evidence |

**CELTA tip:** Always pin meaning to a **context/timeline**, not a memorised “rule only.”

### 4. Modality (keep meanings distinct)

| Modal use | Example | Watch for |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Ability | *She can swim* | *can* vs *could* (past ability / polite) |
| Permission | *You can leave early* | *can* vs *may* (register) |
| Obligation | *You must wear a badge* | *must* vs *have to* vs *should* |
| Prohibition | *You mustn’t smoke here* | vs *don’t have to* (no obligation) |
| Advice | *You should see a doctor* | Softening with *ought to* / *had better* |
| Possibility | *It might rain* | *may/might/could* strength |

### 5. Voice

- Active: *The chef prepared the meal.* (focus on agent)  
- Passive: *The meal was prepared (by the chef).* (focus on action/result; agent optional)  

Form sketch (past simple passive): **be (past) + past participle**

### 6. Inductive vs deductive

- **Deductive:** Rule → examples → practice  
- **Inductive (guided discovery):** Examples → questions → learners formulate rule → practice  

Both are valid; CELTA often values clear guided discovery when time allows.

### 7. Reference tools

Use learner dictionaries, a teacher grammar (e.g. practical reference), and corpora awareness. **Cite sources** in LRT-style work.

## Key terminology glossary

| Term | Working definition |
|------|--------------------|
| **Target language (TL)** | The grammar/lexis you aim to clarify and practise in a lesson |
| **Meaning / form / phonology (MFP)** | Three strands of language analysis for teaching |
| **Concept** | The meaning you want learners to grasp |
| **CCQ** | Concept Checking Question — checks understanding of meaning |
| **Marker sentence** | Clear example sentence showing the TL in context |
| **Auxiliary** | Helping verb (*be, have, do*, modals) |
| **Finite / non-finite** | Verb forms marked for tense/agreement vs infinitives/participles/gerunds |
| **Aspect** | Progressive and/or perfect viewing of a situation |
| **Modality** | Speaker stance: ability, obligation, possibility, etc. |
| **Passive voice** | Structure where object of active becomes subject; agent optional |
| **Guided discovery** | Inductive clarification through questions/examples |
| **Metalanguage** | Language used to talk about language (keep level-appropriate) |

## Worked examples (original)

### Example 1 — Present perfect for experience

**Marker sentence:** *Have you ever worked in a kitchen?*  
**Meaning:** Asks about life experience up to now; time of experience not specified.  
**Form:** *Have/Has + subject + (ever) + past participle …?*  
**Phonology (preview):** *Have you* often /həv jə/; stress on *ever* / *worked*.  
**Anticipated problems:** Learners use past simple only (*Did you ever work…?* may be OK in some varieties but may miss coursebook target); wrong past participle (*worked* vs irregulars).  
**CCQs:**  
- Am I asking about a specific day in the past? *(No)*  
- Is the person’s life experience important now? *(Yes)*  
- Do I need to say *when*? *(No)*  

### Example 2 — *mustn’t* vs *don’t have to*

**Markers:**  
- *You mustn’t park here.* (= prohibited)  
- *You don’t have to park here.* (= not necessary; you can if you want)  

**CCQs for *mustn’t*:** Is it OK to park here? *(No)* Is it against the rules? *(Yes)*  
**CCQs for *don’t have to*:** Is it necessary? *(No)* Is it banned? *(No)*  

### Example 3 — Passive (process focus)

**Marker:** *The applications are checked every Friday.*  
**Meaning:** Focus on the process/result; who checks may be unknown/unimportant.  
**Form:** *am/is/are + past participle* (+ optional *by…*)  
**CCQ:** Do we know who checks them? *(Not said / not important)* Is this a regular action? *(Yes)*

## Practice tasks

**Task 1 — Label M / F ideas**  
For *She’s going to call the client after lunch*:  
(a) Write one sentence explaining **meaning/use**.  
(b) Break down **form**.  
(c) Write **two CCQs**.  

**Task 2 — Error diagnosis**  
Learner says: *I am knowing him for five years.*  
(a) What is the likely intended meaning?  
(b) What form is needed?  
(c) One possible cause?  

**Task 3 — Modals**  
Match: (1) soft advice (2) no obligation (3) prohibition — to:  
A *You don’t have to attend.*  B *You shouldn’t eat that.*  C *You mustn’t touch that.*  

**Task 4 — Mini LRT style**  
Analyse *used to + infinitive* in: *I used to commute by train.*  
Include: meaning, form, one problem, two CCQs.  

**Task 5 — Passive transform**  
Rewrite actively: *The reports were emailed by Marta yesterday.* Then state why a teacher might still teach the passive version.

## On the real CELTA

- Lesson plans require language analysis for grammar lessons (criteria on form/meaning/phonology, anticipated problems, solutions).  
- **Assignment — Language related tasks** is essentially several analyses like the worked examples.  
- In TP, weak CCQs (“Do you understand?”) are a classic tutor note — aim for yes/no or forced-choice questions that only work if the concept is clear.  
- Keep metalanguage light for low levels; still be accurate on your plan.

## Answer key

**Task 1**  
(a) Prior plan/intention to call; arranged in the speaker’s mind; time = after lunch.  
(b) *is/am/are + going to + base verb* (*call*).  
(c) Example CCQs: Has she already decided to call? *(Yes)* Is she calling right now as we speak? *(No)*.  

**Task 2**  
(a) Knowing him for five years up to now (state lasting until present).  
(b) *I’ve known him for five years* (present perfect; *know* not usually continuous).  
(c) Overgeneralising continuous; L1 progressive transfer; confusing *know* with action verbs.  

**Task 3**  
1→B, 2→A, 3→C.  

**Task 4** *(sample)*  
Meaning: past habit/state that is no longer true.  
Form: *used to + base infinitive*.  
Problem: confusion with *be used to + -ing* (be accustomed to); pronunciation /ˈjuːstə/.  
CCQs: Do I still commute by train? *(No)* Was it a regular thing in the past? *(Yes)*.  

**Task 5**  
Active: *Marta emailed the reports yesterday.*  
Why teach passive: to focus on *the reports* / process; agent known but secondary; builds form awareness for texts where agent is omitted.
