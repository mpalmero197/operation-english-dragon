## Learning aims

1. Know the purpose of each of the four CELTA written assignments  
2. Use paraphrased public criteria as a self-check rubric  
3. Practise academic prose, referencing, and word count discipline (750–1000 words typical)  
4. Connect assignments to TP reflection habits

---

## Honest framing

Your **centre designs** the exact briefs. Word limits are typically **750–1000** each; two assignments may sometimes be combined but still assessed as two. This pack gives **original practice** only — not Cambridge scripts.

---

## Assignment overview

### 1. Focus on the learner
Investigate a learner or group: backgrounds, preferences, needs; identify language/skills needs; suggest activities with rationale; use terminology; reference sources.

**Practice:** [focus-on-the-learner-brief.md](../exams/assignment-practice/focus-on-the-learner-brief.md)

### 2. Language related tasks (LRT)
Analyse language items for teaching: meaning, form, phonology, use; problems; CCQs; reference materials; clear accurate writing.

**Practice:** [language-related-tasks-practice.md](../exams/assignment-practice/language-related-tasks-practice.md)

### 3. Language skills related tasks
Work from a text (coursebook-like or authentic-style): identify receptive/productive skills and subskills; design tasks with rationale; terminology; references.

**Practice:** [language-skills-tasks-practice.md](../exams/assignment-practice/language-skills-tasks-practice.md)

### 4. Lessons from the classroom
Reflect on strengths/development needs using feedback and observations of peers/experienced teachers; set specific action points beyond the course.

**Practice:** [lessons-from-classroom-template.md](../exams/assignment-practice/lessons-from-classroom-template.md)

## Core concepts for all four

- Answer the brief — use headings if allowed  
- **Show**, don’t only claim (“e.g. …” with concrete activities)  
- Terminology used **correctly**  
- Reference at least one appropriate source where required (dictionary, methodology text, syllabus-related reading)  
- Clear, accurate written English; proofread  
- Stay near word count; appendices may be allowed by centres for data — follow your centre  

---

## Key terminology glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Rationale** | Reason why an activity suits these learners/aims |
| **Reference / citation** | Crediting a source you used |
| **Action point** | Specific future development step |
| **Needs** | Language/skills gaps relative to goals |
| **Portfolio** | Collection of plans, assignments, reflections |
| **Moderation** | External Cambridge assessor sampling |
| **Academic prose** | Connected formal writing (required for some assignments) |
| **Evidence** | Examples from teaching/observation supporting claims |

## Worked mini-example (rationale snippet)

“Pair information-gap role-plays suit Samira’s reception goals because she must ask for and confirm details under light time pressure. The task recycles *Could you spell that?* which matches her stated difficulty with names, and delayed feedback protects fluency while still upgrading accuracy.”

## On the real CELTA
Assignments are internally assessed and externally moderated. Plagiarism fails candidates. Use your own words; reference ideas. Feedback on drafts may be limited — check centre policy. TP and assignments are **integrated** continuous assessment.
