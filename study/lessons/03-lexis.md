## Learning aims

1. Analyse lexical items for meaning, form, phonology, and use (collocation, register)  
2. Teach vocabulary with clear contexts, not isolated word lists only  
3. Anticipate typical lexical problems and plan concept checks  
4. Use free reference tools (learner dictionaries) effectively for LRT-style work

---

## Core concepts

### What “lexis” covers on CELTA
Words and chunks: single words, multi-word verbs, collocations, fixed expressions, idioms (level-appropriately).

### Meaning
- **Denotation** (core meaning) vs **connotation** (associations)  
- **Sense relations:** synonymy, antonymy, hyponymy (*rose* → *flower*)  
- **Polysemy:** one form, several related meanings (*bank*) — teach the meaning in *your* context  

### Form
Spelling; word class; morphology (prefixes/suffixes); irregular plurals; multi-word verb separability (*turn off the light* / *turn the light off*).

### Use in context
- **Collocation:** *make a decision* / *do homework*  
- **Register/style:** *kids* vs *children* vs *offspring*  
- **Co-text:** surrounding words that constrain choice  

### Teaching implications
Clarify with a clear marker sentence; check meaning; highlight collocation and pronunciation; provide restricted then freer practice; recycle across lessons.

## Key terminology glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Lexis / lexical item** | Vocabulary item (word or chunk) treated as a teaching unit |
| **Collocation** | Words that commonly co-occur |
| **Multi-word verb** | Verb + particle(s), e.g. *look after*, *put up with* |
| **Affixation** | Adding prefixes/suffixes |
| **Compounding** | Combining words (*whiteboard*) |
| **Hyponym / hypernym** | Specific member / broader category |
| **Register** | Formality/style level |
| **Countable / uncountable** | Affects articles and quantifiers |
| **Lexical set** | Related words in a topic field |
| **Recycling** | Meeting/using items again for retention |

## Worked examples (original)

### 1. Collocation — *heavy traffic*
**Meaning:** a lot of vehicles; congestion.  
**Form:** adjective + uncountable noun.  
**Phonology:** stress on *hea-vy TRAF-fic*; /ˈhevi ˈtræfɪk/.  
**Problem:** *strong traffic* / *many traffic*.  
**CCQs:** Are there lots of cars? *(Yes)* Is *traffic* plural here? *(No)*.  

### 2. Multi-word verb — *look after*
**Marker:** *Can you look after my cat this weekend?*  
**Meaning:** take care of.  
**Form:** inseparable verb + particle + object.  
**Problem:** *look for* confusion; inserting *to*.  
**CCQ:** Do I want you to search for the cat or care for it? *(Care for)*.  

### 3. Register — *purchase* vs *buy*
Same core meaning; *purchase* more formal/written. Teach when formal emails are the aim.

## Practice tasks

**Task 1.** Analyse *make progress* (MFP + one problem + two CCQs).  
**Task 2.** Which is odd collocation? *do a mistake / make a mistake / commit a crime* — fix the odd one.  
**Task 3.** For *unfortunately*: word class, typical sentence position, one concept check.  
**Task 4.** Design a 5-minute controlled practice for the set: *book a table, order a meal, leave a tip*.

## Answer key

**Task 1 (sample):** Meaning = improve/advance. Form = *make* + uncountable *progress*. Phonology: stress on *PRO-gress* (noun). Problem: *do progress*. CCQs: Am I improving? *(Yes)* Can I say *a progress*? *(No)*.  

**Task 2.** Odd: *do a mistake* → *make a mistake*.  

**Task 3.** Adverb; often sentence-initial or before adjective/main clause comment; CCQ: Is the news good? *(No)*.  

**Task 4.** Examples: matching verbs–nouns; gap-fill dialogue at a restaurant; reorder words — any restricted practice recycling the three chunks.
