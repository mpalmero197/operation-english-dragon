## Learning aims

1. Use a phonemic chart at CELTA level (recognise/transcribe common sounds)  
2. Teach word stress and notice sentence stress / weak forms  
3. Identify features of connected speech relevant to listening and pronunciation  
4. Include phonology in language analyses (plans & LRT)

---

## Core concepts

### Phonemes
Distinctive sounds. Teachers use **phonemic symbols** (IPA-based ELT charts), not “spelling sounds.”

Useful pairs to know well: /ɪ/–/iː/, /æ/–/ʌ/, /ɒ/–/ɔː/, /θ/–/s/, /v/–/w/, /ʃ/–/tʃ/, /ŋ/–/n/.

### Word stress
One syllable usually stronger in citation form: *phoTOgraph* vs *photoGRAPHic*. Wrong stress harms intelligibility.

### Sentence stress & weak forms
Content words often stressed; function words often weaken: *to* /tə/, *can* /kən/, *have* /həv/.

### Connected speech (recognition + light production)
| Feature | Simple idea | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Linking** | Final consonant → vowel | *an_apple* |
| **Elision** | Sound dropped | *next please* /neks pliːs/ |
| **Assimilation** | Sound becomes more like neighbour | *good boy* → /gʊb bɔɪ/ (approx.) |
| **Intrusion** | Extra linking sound (BrE often /r/, /j/, /w/) | *see_it* /siːjɪt/ |

### Intonation
Rising/falling patterns for questions, lists, attitudes — teach communicatively, not with heavy theory.

### Teaching tips
Model → highlight (board stress bubbles / phonemes) → drill (choral → individual) → practise in chunks → recycle in freer speaking. Keep drills short and purposeful.

## Key terminology glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Phoneme** | Meaning-distinguishing sound unit |
| **Phonemic chart** | Visual layout of English sounds for ELT |
| **Voiced / voiceless** | Vocal cord vibration vs not (/v/ vs /f/) |
| **Word stress** | Stronger syllable in a word |
| **Schwa /ə/** | Weak central vowel, very common in unstressed syllables |
| **Weak form** | Reduced pronunciation of function words |
| **Minimal pair** | Words differing by one sound (*ship/sheep*) |
| **Connected speech** | How sounds change in natural stretches |
| **Intelligibility** | How understandable speech is |
| **Drill** | Controlled repetition practice |

## Worked examples (original)

### 1. Minimal pair teaching
Contrast /ɪ/ vs /iː/: *ship* /ʃɪp/ vs *sheep* /ʃiːp/. CCQ via pictures: Which one is the animal?

### 2. Stress shift
*REcord* (noun) vs *reCORD* (verb). Mark stress; learners sort cards.

### 3. Grammar phonology
*I would have gone* → often /aɪdəvgɒn/ or similar reduction. Anticipated problem: learners write what they hear incorrectly; teach written form separately from fluent pronunciation awareness.

## Practice tasks

**Task 1.** Transcribe with phonemes if you can, or describe: stress in *computer*, *information*, *Japanese*.  
**Task 2.** Identify weak forms likely in: *What do you want to do?*  
**Task 3.** Name the connected-speech feature: *pick it up* (final consonant + vowel).  
**Task 4.** Write one pronunciation focus + drill idea for teaching *Should have* + past participle (regret).

---

## On the real CELTA
Plans should note phonology for TL (stress, weak forms, endings). Listening lessons benefit when you pre-teach “what they’ll hear.” Don’t aim for native-identical accents — aim for clarity.
